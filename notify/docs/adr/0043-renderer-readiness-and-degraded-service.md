# Renderer readiness and degraded service

Graphical startup waits up to ten seconds for the banner renderer's first processed window redraw before importing or recovering notification history. Thread creation and application-state construction are too early: window/graphics initialization can still fail afterward. A renderer-owned lifecycle channel carries starting, ready and terminal stopped states behind the presenter interface; synchronous presenters and headless mode need no independent readiness signal. This milestone proves event-loop readiness, not successful compositor presentation or physically visible pixels.

Once daemon startup completes, renderer exit leaves the notification service running in a degraded state. Committed receipts, sender lifecycles and Notification Center controls remain usable; presentation health reports the exit even while idle. We require an explicit daemon restart to restore banners for now, rather than terminating the service and discarding live sender identities/transient notifications just to trigger systemd restart. There is no automatic renderer replacement or replay guarantee in this decision. Readiness is checked again before opening history: exit during the intervening startup work can still abort startup, and an import already published after first readiness is not rolled back. This extends [ADR 0030](0030-mutation-commit-and-effects.md)'s retirement policy without changing advertised capabilities or display-based expiration.

Startup timeout/cancellation and last-presenter retirement close the update transport, release pending callbacks and prevent late readiness from reviving it. A responsive renderer receives a shutdown request when that transport ends. A blocked native renderer thread cannot be forcibly joined by this mechanism; complete shutdown draining, in-process recovery and real Wayland/Niribar graphical acceptance remain separate work.
